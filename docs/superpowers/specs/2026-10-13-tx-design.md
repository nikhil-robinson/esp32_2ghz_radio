# ESP-SDR phase 2: transmit — design

Status: approved in chat 2026-10-13 · Branch: `s3-radio-lib`

## Goal

Transmit from the ESP32-S3 with `esp_sdr`: a carrier, raw 2-FSK/GFSK bits,
BLE advertising packets and nRF24-style ShockBurst packets. Verified on the
two-board rig with the board's own receiver and an independent host decoder,
and for BLE with a stock scanner.

## Decisions

* **Mode at init.** `esp_sdr_init(ESP_SDR_MODE_RX | ESP_SDR_MODE_TX)`. Calls of
  the other mode return `ESP_ERR_INVALID_STATE`. No RX/TX switching: nothing in
  scope needs a reply, and our measured ~4 ms turnaround is too slow for ACKs
  (ESB 130 µs, BLE T_IFS 150 µs). A TXRX mode is future work (see below).
  The serial firmware switches mode with `SET_MODE`, which reboots the board.
* **Modulator.** CPU busy-loop on core 0 with interrupts masked, writing the
  PHY tone generator frequency field `0x60006040[9:0]` (156.25 kHz per step)
  at absolute cycle-count deadlines from a register table built on the heap
  beforehand. Proven in the spike at 250k/1M/2M. Interrupts are back on
  between packets. Carrier is off (bit 18 cleared) between packets.
* **GFSK.** Optional Gaussian (BT 0.5) shaping: 4 sub-steps per bit at every
  rate (30 CPU cycles per write at 2 Mbps; 2 sub-steps were measurably worse).
  Deviation is rounded to whole 156.25 kHz steps; the fine bits in
  `0x60006050` are not used yet. The BLE and nRF24 helpers send plain FSK.
* **Tuning.** The LO is put exactly on the carrier with the receiver's direct
  PLL path (`set_rf_freq_offset`); the tone field carries only ±deviation.
  The 6/5 divider is a receive path, so TX covers 2210–2790 MHz at most.
* **Regions.** The library defaults to the 2.4 GHz ISM band. Opt-in, with a
  logged warning: `HAM` (2300–2450 MHz, licensed amateurs) and `UNRESTRICTED`
  (2210–2790 MHz, shielded/lab use). The whole occupied band (carrier ±
  deviation) must lie inside the region.
* **Framing** is pure C (`src/tx/packet.c`) and host-tested.
  * BLE ADV_NONCONN_IND: preamble, access address 0x8E89BED6, header, AdvA,
    AdvData (≤ 31 B), CRC-24 (init 0x555555), whitening from the channel
    index; LSB first. Channels 37/38/39. Deviation ±312.5 kHz (2 steps).
  * nRF24 ShockBurst (no ESB, no PCF): preamble 0x55/0xAA chosen from the
    first address bit, 3–5 address bytes (given in air order), payload
    (≤ 32 B), CRC-8 (0x07, init 0xFF) or CRC-16 (CCITT, init 0xFFFF) over
    address + payload, or no CRC; MSB first. Channel = 2400 + n MHz, 0..125.
    Deviation ±156.25 kHz at 250k/1M, ±312.5 kHz at 2M.
* **Repetition** in the library: `count` packets with `gap_us` between them.

## Verification

* Host: framing (CRCs, whitening, preamble, bit order), region limits, and
  `tools/demod.py` self-test on synthetic GFSK.
* Hardware (`tests/hw/tx_rf.py`, TX board → RX board capture at 16 Msps, decoded
  by `tools/demod.py` written from the datasheets, independent of the C code):
  carrier frequency, deviation and sign, nRF24 at 250k/1M/2M with every CRC
  length, BLE with valid CRC-24. BLE is also checked with a stock NimBLE scanner.

## Results (2026-10-13, two ESP32-S3 boards, IDF 5.3.5)

* Tone-field sign after `esp_sdr_init(TX)`: a positive field raises the
  frequency (the rftest-mode spike saw the opposite).
* The first tone-register write after a pause can stall 250+ cycles; one
  dummy write before the bit clock starts removes it (steady lateness 4 cycles).
* `tests/hw/tx_rf.py`: carrier, deviation (156/312/469 kHz) and all 18 nRF24
  cases (250k/1M/2M x CRC 0/1/2 x FSK/GFSK) pass at >= 90 % of the packets
  that fit in the capture windows; BLE 37/39 pass, 38 passed in a targeted
  rerun (37/40 captures) after one run at 77 % with a busy band.
* Stock NimBLE scanner (ESP-IDF, unmodified): 31-32 % of adverts received on
  each channel against a ~33 % ceiling (it hops 37/38/39).
* Frequency offset between the boards grew from 2 to 10-21 ppm during the
  session (same ppm at every frequency, i.e. a reference clock). Two boards
  cannot tell which one moved; needs a reference. Within BLE/nRF24 tolerance.

## Future work

* TXRX mode: first a spike to shorten the RX entry (`prepare_rx` waits 3 ms).
  Only near 150 µs would ACKs become possible.
* Using the closed BLE controller as a fast packet engine for non-BLE formats
  (custom access address, whitening off), as done on nRF52: research.
* TX power over the whole range, fine deviation bits, power calibration.
